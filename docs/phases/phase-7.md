## 1. Scope

Builds on phase 6 (`docs/phases/phase-6.md`): `account.kind = wallet` is already in the CHECK (D156, refused at creation until now), `asset_movement` already carries `network`, `tx_hash`, `fee_quantity` and `movement_hash` (D166, D175, D178), `transfer_link` exists with three kinds (D162), the `price` table and `rules/valuation.price_on` are live (D167), and the exchange withdrawals wait as open legs for the wallet side (D175).

- **Wallets as accounts** (D179): one `wallet` account per wallet (a hardware wallet with its addresses on several chains is one account; the separate Solana wallet is another), with owner shares (D157) and a `wallet_address` row per chain. Addresses are public data the app must query, so the plaintext lives on `wallet_address` only; everywhere else they are masked and HMAC'd like an IBAN, never in an event, a log, a card or a prompt.
- **Chains and assets as data** (D180): a chain registry `rules/chain/chains.yaml` (five chains at the start: Bitcoin, Ethereum, Arbitrum One, BNB Smart Chain, Solana; any other EVM network is one registry row away) and an on-chain asset identity by `(chain, contract)`, never by the ticker, which spam tokens copy.
- **One `ChainSource` per chain, and no provider the app cannot lose** (D181, D192): Bitcoin over the Esplora API (mempool.space and Blockstream are interchangeable, and the API is self-hostable), Ethereum and Arbitrum over Etherscan V2 (free tier; EVM has no standard RPC call for an address's history, so the stored originals are the safeguard), BNB Smart Chain **balance-only** over its public RPC (Luis, 13 Nov 2026: no NodeReal key; BscScan's API is deprecated and Etherscan V2 lists BSC as paid), Solana over **standard JSON-RPC** against a configurable URL (the public endpoint, Luis's Helius Developer plan, or any other provider), movements derived by code from each transaction's balance deltas; Helius's parsed descriptions are at most labels, never a decision input. Keys and URLs are container secrets, never in a trace. A daily sync job, and the provider's answer stored as the sync's original when it brings a new movement (D182), so nothing already read depends on any provider.
- **Movements by code** (D183–D186): every transaction that touches an own address becomes one `movement_group` with its movements, classified by deterministic rules and protocol decoders kept as data (`decoders.yaml`); the fee is always its own movement; a transfer between own addresses, own wallets and own exchanges is linked by its transaction hash (D175's `chain` link) or by asset, quantity and window; swaps, wraps, bridges, rewards, airdrops and spam have their rules; what no rule explains is a Zu prüfen card with a question and the answers that fit. **No model call anywhere in this phase** (D189), so no eval case and no consent switch.
- **Lots and the holding period on read** (D187): `rules/lots.py` runs FIFO per account and asset over confirmed movements and links, every lot keeping its acquisition day and cost through transfers, wraps and bridges; the countdown „steuerfrei ab“ by §23 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 EStG with the day arithmetic of §108 AO; a cost that cannot be priced is a to-do, never 0 €.
- **Reconciliation per address** (D186) and the **year-end holdings statement** (D188): Σ movements per address and asset equals the chain's balance at the sync's block, else a card and nothing valued past the last reconciled sync; the 31 Dec statement is computed on read and exported as an immutable snapshot.
- **Screens**: Konten gains the Krypto › Wallets group (mockup 08), Anlagen gains its Krypto tab with lots and „steuerfrei ab“ (mockup 09c), Zu prüfen gains the wallet cards.

**Not in this phase**: Anlage SO and KAP, the Freigrenze meter, §22 Nr. 3 rewards against €256, the lot-method election (FIFO is the only method here; the election is phase 8's rule data, design §4; phase 8 D201 offers no other method), the position on LP deposits (stored as `lp_*` movements, decided in phase 8), perpetual futures and bot wallets (none yet, 6 Nov 2026), Ethereum L2s beyond Arbitrum until a wallet address on one exists, NFTs (an ERC-721/1155 or Metaplex NFT that reaches an own address is recorded as `spam` unless the user promotes it; its valuation is out of scope), Lightning, and any write access to a chain (never: the Security bullet). Phase 8's spec (merged 16 Nov 2026, PR #384) decides the items left to it: the forms (D198–D206), the lot method (D201, FIFO only), the LP position (D209, the household's position B, Q-O97), NFTs (D213); perpetual futures stay out (D219 deferred, Q-O102).

## 4. Wallets and addresses (`services/wallets.py`, `rules/chain/address.py`), cases in `chains.yaml`

A wallet is created on Konten › „Konto anlegen“ with Kontoart „Wallet“ (provider, owner shares with the reason, no IBAN), then its addresses on the wallet page: „Adresse hinzufügen“ (chain from the registry, the address pasted). The service validates the format (`rules/chain/address.validate(chain, address)` → the normalised form or `wallet.address_invalid` with the chain's expected shape in plain words), masks and hashes it, refuses one registered on another own account (`wallet.address_taken`), writes the row and the event `wallet.address_added` (masked form, chain, label), and enqueues the first sync (D182). Retiring an address sets `active = false` (an event; its movements stay). A hardware wallet's EVM address is added once per chain the user wants read: the form offers „Auch auf Arbitrum und BNB Smart Chain lesen“ when an Ethereum address is added, which creates the three rows with one click, each syncing on its own.

The wallet page lists the addresses (masked, chain, label, kind, last sync with its block and „stimmt mit der Chain überein“ or the card), the balances per asset from the last reconciled sync, the hidden tokens (D187), the open cards, and „Jetzt abrufen“. Nothing on it prints an address in full; „kopieren“ copies the full address from the row to the clipboard client-side (a `GET /wallets/<id>/addresses/<aid>/plain` that is never cached and writes an event `wallet.address_revealed`), so a user can verify it against the hardware wallet without the app ever displaying it in a list.

## 5. Chain sources and the sync (`services/chain/`, `jobs/tasks.sync_wallets`), fixtures `chains.yaml`, `evm_tx.yaml`, `solana_tx.yaml`, `btc_utxo.yaml`

`services/chain/base.py` (the protocol of D181), `bitcoin_esplora.py`, `evm_etherscan.py`, `evm_rpc_balance.py`, `solana_rpc.py` and the optional `solana_helius_labels.py`, each a small module that turns the provider's JSON into `ChainTx` rows (or, for Solana, into the per-asset deltas of D181 (d)) and knows nothing of movements; every module takes its base URL from `Settings`, so a provider is swapped by one variable (D192); `services/chain/sync.py` runs one address: cursor → pages → `rules/chain/normalise` → `rules/chain/classify` (D183) → groups and movements written `proposed` or `confirmed` by D186's routing → links by `rules/chain/match_transfer` (D184) → balances → reconciliation (D186) → the `wallet_sync` row and, when new movements or a new question came, the stored original (D182 (3), amended 16 Nov 2026 with migration 0044). The fixtures hold the provider records as recorded JSON (invented addresses and hashes in the real shapes) and the `ChainTx` each source must produce from them, so a provider's format change fails a test before it corrupts a ledger; `chains.yaml` replays the same records through two base URLs (D192). Rate pacing per source is a `sleep` injected as in `CoinGeckoDaily(sleep=)`.

The task `sync_wallets` (periodic, 06:45 UTC, `periodic_id = "sync_wallets"`) syncs every active address of every wallet whose source is configured, oldest sync first, within a budget per run (Solana: `HAUSBUCH_SOLANA_RPC_RPS` × 600 requests, so about 2,400 on the public endpoint's per-method cap and 24,000 on the Developer plan, each `getTransaction` one request; Etherscan: 2,000 calls; Esplora: 600 requests; BSC: one balance call per registry token) and leaves the rest for the next day with `wallet.sync_deferred` on the wallet page, never a card. The first sync of a wallet with years of history may take days on the public endpoint and hours on a paid one; the page shows „Verlauf wird geladen: bis <date>“. The Solana history is read **in full, back to the address's first signature**, and classified in full (Luis, 13 Nov 2026), so the lots at 1 Jan 2026 rest on every earlier movement; if the configured URL cannot serve the oldest signatures (Q-O93), the sync records the oldest slot it reached in `wallet_sync` and the wallet page offers „Anderen RPC-Anbieter eintragen“ with the runbook step, and as the last resort the typed opening statement of §18 (5).

## 6. Movements and classification (`rules/chain/classify.py`, `rules/chain/decoders.yaml`), fixtures `decoders.yaml`, `swap_group.yaml`, `spam.yaml`

6. **A spam token** credited to A's EVM address, contract unknown to the registry, A paid no fee → `spam`, confirmed, hidden, no price asked (D187 (1)); the same token later promoted with „Das ist echt“ → `airdrop`, review.

9. **A bridge**: 0.5 ETH out on Ethereum to the canonical Arbitrum bridge at 10:00, 0.5 ETH in on Arbitrum at 10:20 → `bridge` proposed with the pair for review the first time; confirmed → the lot moves to ETH-on-Arbitrum unchanged; „Künftig immer so“ → `chain_pair` row, later bridges over that pair confirmed by the matcher.

11. **An unknown program call** that moved a token out and nothing in → `contract_call`, review with the template answers; „Bezahlung“ → `disposal` priced on the day, FIFO consumes the oldest lot.
12. **A Bitcoin spend** (D185): inputs 0.05 BTC from `bc1q…k7w2` (own), outputs 0.02 BTC to `bc1q…p9d3` (foreign) and 0.0299 BTC to `bc1q…z4r8` (unregistered), fee 0.0001 → card „Ist …z4r8 Ihr Wechselgeld?“; yes → the address is registered as `change`, the movement is `out` 0.02 BTC plus `fee` 0.0001, the 0.0299 is no movement; no → `out` 0.0499 BTC plus the fee. ### 15.1 Readings and results (filled as the steps land)

**Step 0 (merged and deployed 15 Nov 2026, PR #366, images 169d42394cd1, migration 0041 applied).**

*Q-O92, the holding-period day arithmetic (read 15 Nov 2026 on gesetze-im-internet.de).*

- §23 Abs. 1 Satz 1 Nr. 2 EStG: „Veräußerungsgeschäfte bei anderen Wirtschaftsgütern, bei denen der Zeitraum zwischen Anschaffung und Veräußerung nicht mehr als ein Jahr beträgt.“
- §108 Abs. 1 AO: „Für die Berechnung von Fristen und für die Bestimmung von Terminen gelten die §§ 187 bis 193 des Bürgerlichen Gesetzbuchs entsprechend, soweit nicht durch die Absätze 2 bis 5 etwas anderes bestimmt ist.“
- §187 Abs. 1 BGB: „Ist für den Anfang einer Frist ein Ereignis oder ein in den Lauf eines Tages fallender Zeitpunkt maßgebend, so wird bei der Berechnung der Frist der Tag nicht mitgerechnet, in welchen das Ereignis oder der Zeitpunkt fällt.“
- §188 Abs. 2 BGB: „… endigt im Falle des § 187 Abs. 1 mit dem Ablauf desjenigen Tages der letzten Woche oder des letzten Monats, welcher durch seine Benennung oder seine Zahl dem Tage entspricht, in den das Ereignis oder der Zeitpunkt fällt …“; Abs. 3: „Fehlt bei einer nach Monaten bestimmten Frist in dem letzten Monat der für ihren Ablauf maßgebende Tag, so endigt die Frist mit dem Ablauf des letzten Tages dieses Monats.“
- These four agree with D188 (3) as written: the acquisition day not counted, the year ending with the day bearing the same number, a sale on that day „nicht mehr als ein Jahr“, 29 Feb ending with 28 Feb. `holding_period.yaml` follows D188 (3) unchanged.
- **Settled 15 Nov 2026 (from two tax advisers' articles, kanzlei-arndt.com and steuerberater-pressler.de, „Fristberechnung bei der Veräußerung von Kryptowährungen“):** §108 Abs. 3 AO („Fällt das Ende einer Frist auf einen Sonntag, einen gesetzlichen Feiertag oder einen Sonnabend, so endet die Frist mit dem Ablauf des nächstfolgenden Werktags.“) does not reach the §23 holding period, which is a period of time, not a deadline for an act. A holding period ending on a weekend or holiday is therefore not extended: it is computed by §108 Abs. 1 AO with §§ 187 Abs. 1, 188 Abs. 2 and 3 BGB only, and D188 (3) stands as written, its own example (acquired 1 Jan 2027, the year ending Saturday 1 Jan 2028) tax-free from Sunday 2 Jan 2028. The BMF letter's Rn. 55 is still not read. (Until 15 Nov 2026 this bullet left the question open and recommended a countdown note that the tax-free day might move to the next working day; no such note is needed.)

*Q-O95, the Solana shapes.* The official reference (`solana.com/docs/rpc/http/gettransaction`) answered 429 again on 15 Nov 2026. *Readings and deviations (each one a reading of the spec, not a new decision):*

1. **`reminder_ack.kind` (§11, D190)** does not exist. 0019 has a pattern CHECK on `reminder_key` that `crypto.holdings_statement` already meets, so the migration changes nothing there.
2. **`movement_group`'s `UNIQUE (workspace_id, account_id, chain, tx_hash, id)`** is kept as §11 wrote it. A superseding row for the same transaction is a new id; „one live group per transaction“ is the service's rule (step 2), not a constraint.
3. **The triggers named in §11, as built:**
   - `group_forward` lets a group move only from proposed to confirmed and set `superseded_by` once;
   - `wallet_sync_finish_once` changes only the outcome columns, and only while the run is `running`;
   - `wallet_address_of_a_wallet` refuses a non-wallet account;
   - `wallet_address_label_only` lets only `active` and `label` change.
4. **Address kinds.** `change` is allowed on Bitcoin only, `stake` on Solana only. Both are table CHECKs.
5. **`DecoderKind`** (`swap, wrap, bridge, stake, reward, lp`) is a subset of `GroupKind`.
6. **`chain_pair`** holds each side as its chain plus an `asset` id (`from_chain`/`from_asset_id`, `to_chain`/`to_asset_id`, FKs to `asset`), not a contract text. A CHECK says a bridge joins two chains and a wrap stays on one, and only a bridge has a `max_fee_share`.
7. **Wrapped coins.** wSOL (Solana) and USDT0 (Arbitrum) are as described in the token paragraph. On Solana a wrap is the Token program's `syncNative`, so its `decoders.yaml` row names a `method`; a row with `method: null` matches any call to its program. Reading for step 2: a lookup tries the program with the instruction's method before the program alone.
8. **EIP-55.** The repository has no keccak library (Python's `sha3_256` is not keccak-256), so the checksum cases in `tests/fixtures/phase7/chains.yaml` are `pending` until step 1 adds one. Since D180 amended (b) (15 Nov 2026) nothing stored depends on it: EVM is stored lower-case, and whether step 1 adds a keccak library to check and show the EIP-55 form is step 1's open point.
9. **Precision.** Chain amounts stay integer base units until a movement is written. `asset_movement.quantity` is `numeric(28,10)`, so an 18-decimal amount loses digits below 10⁻¹⁰ there. **Recommendation:** widen `quantity` to scale 18 in step 2's migration rather than rounding, since D185 reconciles to the unit. **Decided 15 Nov 2026 (the step 0 review):** not in 0041; step 2 has its own migration slot in §16 (`asset_movement.quantity` numeric(38,18), `asset.decimals` 0..18, the `Quantity` wire alias with them), landing before step 2 writes its first movement. `evm_tx.yaml`'s `fee_below_numeric_scale` and `schemas/chain.py`'s docstring say the same.
10. **Uniqueness.**
    - `asset`: a symbol lookup without a chain must say `chain IS NULL`, because the partial unique index is on `COALESCE(chain, '')`.
    - Contract uniqueness is case-sensitive. The service stores the EIP-55 or base58 form as the registry writes it. **Superseded 15 Nov 2026** by D180 amended (b): an EVM contract or address is stored lower-case and its mixed-case form is refused; a Solana mint stays as written.
    - Since D180 amended (a), the symbol index covers only rows without a contract, so a lookup by symbol alone could also meet a chain token reading the same symbol: the three phase 6 lookups gain `chain IS NULL` in step 2 (§16).
11. **Events.** The three new `event.entity_type` values exist. The event types themselves are written by the steps that emit them.
12. **Decimal strings.** Two §12 fields use their own aliases, so they travel as decimal strings like every other figure (phase 1 amendment 2, `test_money_strings`):
    - `BridgePair.max_fee_share` is `FeeShare`, the `chain_pair.max_fee_share` column's numeric(5,4) in [0, 1).
    - `Lot.cost_per_unit` is `CostPerUnit`, 20 digits with 8 places like `PriceEur`, but 0 allowed, since a reward lot may carry no cost.

    §12 states neither constraint.
13. **OpenAPI.** `AssetMovement` gained `group_id`, `address_id` and `index` (nullable), which changed `openapi.json` and the generated frontend types. `GroupRoutingInput` is not in the OpenAPI.
14. **`movement_import.format = chain_sync`** passes the CHECK even for an import typed by hand. The services refuse it (step 2).

1. **A token is its contract, its symbol a label** (D180 amended (a)). 0041 replaces 0025's `asset_symbol_check` with a chain-aware one, widens `asset_check` so a contract meets the symbol-or-ISIN requirement, and makes the symbol index partial `WHERE symbol IS NOT NULL AND contract_address IS NULL`; the downgrade restores 0025's two CHECKs exactly. `TokenSymbol` (1–32 characters, no control characters) is the registry rows' and the fixtures' symbol type. **Step 2a, the chain core and sync loop (PR #375, merged and deployed 16 Nov 2026, images bbc6c0fa8b73, no migration; this heading said „not merged“ until then).** `services/chain/sync.py` (`sync_address`, `sync_wallets`), the routing codes and the error codes; the families plug in through `services/chain/base.factory_for`.

*Readings and the review's fixes (16 Nov 2026, each one a reading of the spec, not a new decision):*

1. **„Both legs have a price for the day“ (D186 (1)) is read as „both legs are priceable“**: a leg is `priced` when its asset is the chain's native coin or a registry-listed token, decided when the asset is created, not by a price row for that day. The net is D188's rule that an unpriced cost is a to-do, never 0 €: a confirmed swap whose day has no price yet leaves its lot's cost a to-do until a price is fetched or typed, so nothing is valued at zero. This also answers step 0 item 6's open point for routing (a Solana token reaches its price through `priced_as`, which the assets PR built).
2. **A chain whose reader is not built is skipped.** `factory_for`'s `NotImplementedError` (Bitcoin until its family's `make_source` lands) gives the outcome `wallet.source_unavailable` with no `wallet_sync` row, like an unset key. Any other failure of an address's start ends that address only; the next one runs.
3. **Deferred is the budget's verdict, never the cursor's.** A spent budget after any page, or after the balance calls, ends the run `deferred` with no balance; an address the budget never reached writes no row (outcome `wallet.sync_deferred`, status none), so it runs first the next day and its first sync, which never confirms, is still ahead of it. Until this fix a refused page returned an unmoved cursor, which read as the tip, and the next address's first sync was recorded as done with nothing read.
4. **An unlisted token has no symbol** (D180 amended (a)), spam or not: the symbol the chain tells is kept only inside its name („Token Vote…1111 (nennt sich BTC)“), with every control character (C0, C1, format) dropped and capped at 32 characters, so a token calling itself „BTC“ never meets a price by that symbol.
5. **One transaction never blocks the address's history.** Quantities are converted from their digits both ways, never through 28-digit `Decimal` arithmetic, and never rounded. A spam leg numeric(38,18) cannot hold is left out (counted as `spam_dropped`, its asset never created). A non-spam transaction Hausbuch cannot store (a quantity beyond the column, two legs of different assets on one (address, index, role), which `_plan` treats as a classifier error, or a row the database refuses inside its savepoint) becomes a proposed group with no movements and no question, its event's reason `routing.chain_unstorable` (card text beside the constant in `rules/review.py`), written once per transaction; the cursor moves past it, the run's original is stored, and its difference stays `reconciled` false, never „wartet auf Antwort“. This also does the EVM entry's first wiring item below.
6. **„Wartet auf Antwort“ is narrow.** Only the (address, asset) pairs an open question names wait: its legs' assets on its address, or, for a question without legs (a Bitcoin change card), the native coin of the address whose run asked it (found through the event's `sync_id`). A difference in any other asset is a mismatch with the whole difference stored. No migration.
7. **Account scope (Bitcoin), pinned by a test:** when the account's last address of the run fails, the earlier rows keep `reconciled` null and the failed row is what the wallet shows; `WalletSyncRun`'s docstring says the account's verdict is the newest non-null row among the account's addresses on that chain.
8. **A leg joining a confirmed group is routed on its own**: another own address's leg of a transaction already written (a fee-only group confirmed by code) is proposed on that address's first sync, and confirmed only when D186 (1) would confirm it.

*Not done here:* the exchange and depot sums' wide decimal context (the assets entry's deferral above) and a classifier exception, which still ends its address's run.

**Step 2, Solana (PR #372, merged 16 Nov 2026; called by the app since PR #385, deployed the same day).** The pure readings of `rules/chain/solana.py` and the source `services/chain/solana_rpc.py`, with the review's fixes of 16 Nov 2026. Item 1 departs from D181 (d)'s wording; the others are readings of D181 (d), D182, D183 and D186, not new decisions:

1. **Rent is read from the record, never fetched.** D181 (d) says the rent the sender pays for a recipient's new token account is a SOL outflow „beside a token leg“ equal to the rent-exempt minimum „read from `getMinimumBalanceForRentExemption` once per sync“. As built, a new account's rent is the lamports the record shows it holding after the transaction, so no such call is made and no token leg needs to move beside it: the record alone says which account is new and what it holds. The rent of a third party's account the own fee payer opens joins the `fee` movement.
2. **A new foreign wSOL account.** When the own payer opens a wrapped-SOL account of someone else's and sends SOL into it, the account holds the rent plus the SOL sent. Only the rent joins the fee; the SOL sent stays a leg (a send of 3 SOL is an out-leg of 3 SOL and a fee of 5,000 + 2,039,280 lamports, a card „transfer.unmatched_leg“). Before the fix the whole amount was folded into the fee and the leg vanished. In a swap, the SOL given stays the disposal. Through a program no decoder row reads, the same leg and fee make a contract call (D183 (4)), so that case shows `group.contract_call`, not `transfer.unmatched_leg`.
3. **Rent a third party pays into a new own token account** is a SOL `in` leg of its own, index `rent`, kind `airdrop`: received without anything given, so it makes a lot. In a spam group it is a leg of that hidden group and raises no card (it comes with every spam attack and is about 0.002 SOL); beside a normal receive it is the same `airdrop` leg.
4. **An airdrop by shape.** A listed or held token credited while the own address signed nothing, through a program other than the Token programs and without a decoder row that reads it, is an `airdrop` group; spam by D187 (1) comes first.
5. **Token rows without an owner.** A token balance row normally names its `owner`. When a record carries none, the row is placed by its token account's address against the own token accounts the caller passes (`own_token_accounts`: the sync's `getTokenAccountsByOwner` set plus every token account in the stored groups). A row the set does not hold is someone else's only when the same record places a row of the same mint on an own account (the counterparty of that leg) or when the account's amount did not change; any other such row stays unresolved (amended 16 Nov 2026 by the review of the wiring: before, a row on an account that existed before the transaction was read as someone else's, so a send from an own token account closed before the first sync, in a record from before the RPC named owners, was booked by code as its fee alone and the token leg was lost). An unresolved row, or a Token program call with no token balance rows at all, raises the new code `wallet.record_unreadable`, whose message names the signature only; the RPC source raises it as a `ChainSourceError`, like every other source error. A leg is never dropped silently. A failed transaction's token rows are not read at all: it reverted everything but its fee.
6. **The leg index (D182 (2)) uses only what every jsonParsed record carries:** the top-level instructions' positions and program ids, never a parsed `type` or `info`, so a provider that leaves `info` out cannot change a movement's hash. A token leg's index is the first top-level Token program call, a SOL leg's the first System program call; else the first call that is not a compute-budget, memo or account-creation call; else `0`. Besides these instruction indexes there are `fee` and `rent`, and a second leg on the same index and role becomes `<index>.2`, `<index>.3`. Every `solana_tx.yaml` expectation already followed this rule, so no fixture changed.
7. **The RPC source.** `getSignaturesForAddress` is paged back until an empty page, not until a short one, since a provider may answer fewer than the limit while older signatures remain. Error −32001 (block cleaned up) joins the ledger errors that stop a sync with the slot reached (Q-O93), from `getTransaction` and from `getBlockTime`. `transactions()` and `chain_tx()` take every own address of the wallet and the own token accounts; their `transfers` and `fee` are right only with them, and the classifier reads the stored `raw` record, never those fields.
8. **Not built yet in step 2:** the sync that writes the groups, the routes, the screens and the end-to-end test. They wait for the shared group writer and `rules/review.route_group`, which still raises „not implemented“. `OpenWalletCode` (`schemas/chain.py`) does not list `wallet.record_unreadable`; whether that code becomes a card on the wallet page is decided with the sync. (Since answered by the wiring below: it does not.)

**Step 2, Solana wired into the sync (PR #385, review fixes A–E applied, merged and deployed 16 Nov 2026, images e95c96215f4d, no migration; this heading said „not yet merged“ until then; Q-O162, a first read larger than one day's request budget freezing the address, is open).** `services/chain/solana_rpc.make_source` and `rules/chain/solana.classify_tx` are the family's halves of the core's protocol (`services/chain/base.py`, `rules/chain/classify.py`). The core changed in two places only, both from the review of 16 Nov 2026: a source that sets `reads_stored` is handed the address's stored originals (item 2), and `rules/review.route_group` never books a fee alone that asks a question (item 5). Readings, each of D181 (d), D182, D183, D186 or D192, not new decisions:

1. **What a run lists.** `getSignaturesForAddress` lists only the transactions whose account keys hold the address, so a token received into an own token account names that token account alone (`solana_tx.yaml` `spl_in`). A run lists the address, every token account `getTokenAccountsByOwner` gives it, and every token account a record read in the run names as the address's (a closed one), merged by signature and served oldest first by (slot, signature). A token account found late, below the highest slot served, is listed and served in the same run: from its first transaction when no earlier run stored a record naming it, so a closed token account a record first names in a later run is read in full; from where the cursor says the stored ones are complete otherwise (item 2). The run first fixes the chain's finalized height (`getSlot`) and serves nothing above it, so listings read moments apart agree on where the history ends (one request more per run).
2. **The cursor is `<slot>:<signature>`**, or `<slot>:<signature>;<closed>` after a run the budget cut, and never names a token account. `<slot>` is the lowest slot at which something the run listed from the address or an open token account is still unserved, else the highest slot served; the next run lists the address and its open token accounts again from that slot, inclusive (listings of several accounts have no order inside one slot, so that slot's transactions are read once more and the movement hashes keep them once: one `getTransaction` a day at the tip). `<signature>` is the highest served at that slot, empty while one there waits, so the cursor moves while a run moves on and comes back unchanged at the tip, which ends the core's loop. `<closed>` (empty: from the first) says where the own token accounts found in earlier runs are complete to: a run the budget cut, while a closed token account had listed transactions unserved or was not yet reached, writes it, and the next run then lists every token account the address's stored originals name (by a row's `owner`, or the `_hausbuch` set of item 4) from there, so it finds a closed one without the record that named it. The stored originals are read only then, through `reads_stored` (`services/chain/base.py`), and nothing read from them reaches a cursor, an event or a log; no new column. A page never splits a slot, and a page is never handed back with the cursor it was given while something listed waits and the budget has not refused. A record leaves the listing only once its record and its block time are both in hand, so a budget that refuses `getBlockTime` leaves it for the next run. A cursor this source did not write (the 4-part form of the first wiring included, never in a live row) starts over. Tests: every daily budget from the smallest workable one (pinned: 12 requests for the mixed history) to one past what an unlimited run takes reaches the tip and serves what one unlimited run serves, read the same way, over a mixed history at three page sizes; the 306-transaction history at a sample of six budgets (`tests/test_solana_source.py`); the review's sequences 1 (a), (b), (c) and 4 as regressions; the 306-transaction sequence through the database, every transaction stored. **Limits, as built:** the smallest workable daily budget is one run's start (the height, the address's listing, both token programs and every own token account's listing, from `<closed>` after a cut) plus one transaction, and at the tip one request more than the run takes, because the core counts a budget used to its last request as spent; below it the address makes no progress and its cursor stays where it is for good. The start after a cut run lists every token account the stored originals name, one request each at least, so the floor grows with them: a wallet that ever held about as many token accounts as its daily budget (2,400 requests on the public endpoint's 4 per second; a run of spam airdrops opens a token account each) freezes on the `;<closed>` cursor after its first cut run (checked 16 Nov 2026 with 30 closed token accounts: a daily budget of 25 froze after the first run, one of 70 reached the tip in nine runs), see Q-O162. **Changed by the sync follow-ups (PR #397, merged and deployed 16 Nov 2026; this said „not merged“ until then; sync follow-ups item 5 below): the start no longer lists the stored token accounts; a sweep lists them one at a time and the cursor carries an index into them, so the floor no longer grows with them (30 closed token accounts: 9 requests; the mixed history's pin moved from 12 to 10).** A listing longer than one run's budget (pages of 1,000 newest first) would never finish. A closed token account named only by records that wrote neither a movement nor a question card (migration 0044 keeps the original for a card too) is not in the stored originals. A record classified before the run learns its token account (a debit served before the record that names the account as the owner's) stays the unreadable card of item 5. The balance is read after the height is fixed, so a transaction between the two shows as a mismatch until the next run. The `wallet_sync.oldest_slot_reached` column stays unwritten, see Q-O93.
3. **Every own address is its own run and is reconciled alone** (`reconcile_scope = "address"`): the deltas are the synced address's only, so a payment between two own addresses is an `out` on one and an `in` on the other, the fee once. This settles the follow-up „the sync passes own addresses and token accounts to `transactions`“ inside the source, without touching `sync.py`. The other own addresses only answer the stake question: a delegation to an address the wallet holds asks nothing.
4. **The stored original is the provider's record**, with one addition: a record whose token balance rows carry no `owner` also stores, under the key `_hausbuch`, the own token accounts the run knew, so a replay with no provider configured reads the same legs (D192). A record whose rows name their owner is stored exactly as the provider sent it.
5. **`wallet.record_unreadable` is not a wallet card code.** A record whose token legs cannot be read is one transaction's card, kind `unknown`, with the question „Hausbuch kann diese Transaktion nicht vollständig lesen. Welche Token hat sie bewegt?“, and the rest of the address is booked. The card **carries its fee** when the address paid it: `rules/review.route_group` books a fee alone by code only when the group asks nothing (`GroupRoutingInput.question`, 16 Nov 2026; an EVM or Bitcoin fee alone asks nothing and is booked as before), so the card waits for its answer and the fee is booked with it. Until then the address's SOL reads „wartet auf Antwort“ and a token the record moved is a mismatch. (The first wiring gave the card no legs at all, since a fee leg would have been booked by code and the question dropped.) An old slot's record that comes back with `meta: null` is the same card; one without `blockTime` takes the listing's time, else `getBlockTime`'s, kept beside the record under `_hausbuch` as `block_time`, and a slot with no time at all fails the run as `wallet.source_failed`.
6. **Budget and errors.** Every request asks the run's `RequestBudget` first; a „no“ ends the page where it stood, never an error, and nothing listed is dropped (item 2). A JSON-RPC or transport failure is `wallet.source_failed` with the HTTP status at most, a 429 `wallet.source_throttled`, a 401/403 `wallet.source_refused`; `host` is the host name alone and the URL is never in an error, a log or a repr. An empty URL is `wallet.key_missing`, which only a settings object built without the check can reach: Settings refuses an empty `HAUSBUCH_SOLANA_RPC_URL` and an unset one is the public endpoint.

**Step 3, EVM (PR #371, merged 16 Nov 2026; wired into `sync_wallets` by PR #386, merged and deployed 16 Nov 2026, see the wiring entry below).** The rules (`rules/chain/evm.py`, `rules/chain/sync_window.py`) and the two sources (`services/chain/evm_etherscan.py`, `services/chain/evm_rpc_balance.py`) are built and replay their fixtures. PR #371 alone did not call them; the wiring below makes `sync_wallets` read Ethereum, Arbitrum and BNB Smart Chain addresses with them (this header said „nothing syncs a wallet yet“ after the sync core had shipped).

*Readings and deviations (each one a reading of the spec, not a new decision):*

1. **A token row's index when Etherscan gives none.** Etherscan's `tokentx` rows (and the NFT lists) carry no `logIndex`, so a row without one gets a synthesised index `log:<16 hex>` (`nft:<16 hex>` for an NFT record), a hash of the row's own fields, with `:<n>` for an identical repeat in the same transaction. This deviates from D182 (2), which names the log index: the index is still stable across re-reads of the same rows (a test reads twice), but a later read through a provider that does give the log index would key the same leg differently. A token row with a `logIndex` keeps it; an NFT record always gets the `nft:` index.
2. **A listed or held token from a third party is a transfer, not an airdrop** (reading (a) of D183 (3)). A registry token or a held token credited by another address in a transaction the address did not sign is (d), a `transfer` with an `uncategorized` leg and `transfer.unmatched_leg`; it is an `airdrop` only when it is minted to the address (sender the zero address) or the address signed the claim. D183 (3)(d) wins over (e) for listed tokens, so a payment received in USDC is never booked as an airdrop.
3. **Own counterparty and exchange hash before spam** (the review of PR #371). D183 (3)(a) (the counterparty is another own address) and (b) (the transaction's hash is named by an own exchange withdrawal) are decided before D187 (1)'s spam test: such a leg is never spam, even for a token the registry does not list and no one holds. With an own counterparty, the other junk legs in the same transaction (an NFT record, a third party's unlisted token) stay spam legs; in a transaction an exchange withdrawal names, every credited leg is the deposit and only an NFT record stays a spam leg. Until this fix an unlisted token withdrawn from an exchange, or moved between two own addresses, was hidden as spam. **Corrected in the wiring's review (finding 1): in the sync, (b) took effect only then.** The wiring's `classify_tx` passed no exchange hashes to `classify_evm`, so an unlisted ERC-20 an exchange sent to an own address was still spam: confirmed by code, hidden, left out of the reconciliation, with no way to repair it. `ClassifyContext.exchange_hashes` now carries the hashes the workspace's own exchange withdrawals name (`asset_movement.tx_hash` of an `internal_transfer_out` on an exchange account, not superseded, read under RLS in `sync._context`), and `classify_tx` passes them. Such a group is an `exchange_transfer`, still proposed until step 5 writes the link (`linked` stays false, the rule order and the spam rule unchanged); a hash is never put into an event or a log line. `test_an_unlisted_token_an_exchange_sent_is_an_exchange_transfer_not_spam` pins it, with a native-coin credit and the unlisted twin no withdrawal names, which stays spam.
4. **A fake token sent out is spam, with no card** (decided by the orchestrator in the review of PR #371, a reading of D187). An out leg of a token that is unlisted and not held, in a transaction the address did not sign (no `txlist` row from the address, so no fee), is a spam leg and raises no card. This covers address poisoning, where a fake token's contract emits `Transfer(victim → lookalike)` in the victim's name. A signed send of the same token, or an out leg of a held token, is not spam.
5. **Quantities are exact.** `to_draft` builds a leg's quantity from the integer's digits and the asset's decimals, with no `Decimal` arithmetic, so the default 28-digit context never rounds it (2²⁵⁶ − 1 stays exact). Whether the quantity fits is the schema's question: `Quantity` (numeric(28,10)) refuses a 29-digit amount or 2²⁵⁶ − 1 with a validation error, as it did before the fix (checked 16 Nov 2026), until step 2's migration widens it (§15.1 step 0 item 9).
6. **Etherscan's `status "0"`.** An answer of `status "0"` with an empty list is „no rows“ only when its message reads „No … found“ („No transactions found“, „No records found“; the per-list wording was not checked against the live API, only Etherscan's documented examples); anything else (a bare `NOTOK` with `[]`) is a refusal, so the sync fails and its cursor does not advance past rows it never saw.

*What the wiring step must do (it is not built here):*

- A spam leg whose quantity cannot be stored (more digits than `asset_movement.quantity` holds, which a spam token's absurd supply easily reaches) must not fail the run: drop the leg or store it capped, never raise for the whole sync.
- `reconcile_balance_only` compares only the assets it is given; the caller must skip an asset with no balance row, or ask `balanceOf` for it, so a token without a balance read is never reported as „coins left“.
- Balances are read straight after the tip, in the same run: Etherscan's `eth_call` at a hex block tag reaches only about 128 blocks back on a non-archive node, so a balance read long after the tip is refused.
- `wallet.source_failed` (a source answered something unreadable, a 5xx, a timeout) is defined locally as `evm_http.SOURCE_FAILED`; it must join `rules/errors.py` with the other `wallet.*` codes.

*Readings and decisions:*

1. **A forward cursor over a backward API** (D182 (1)). Esplora pages an address's history newest first from the last txid of the page before, so its own position cannot be where the next run starts. The cursor is `"<h>"`, every transaction through height h read, or `"<h>:<f>:<txid>"` while a run the budget deferred walks the window (h, f] back from that txid. Nothing above the tip − 6 is read (D182 (4)); a run with nothing new final sends one request, the tip.
2. **The balance is the one at the same height** (D182 (4), D186 (2)): Esplora's tip balance with the later blocks' transactions backed out (`utxo.balance_at`), for every own address of the account (`reconcile_scope = "account"`, D185 (3)). A block found between the two reads makes that run's figure wrong by its transaction; the next run reads it right.
3. **The URL setting is `HAUSBUCH_ESPLORA_URL`** (`Settings.esplora_urls`, one URL or several, tried in order); the module's earlier reading of a single `esplora_url` is still accepted. The budget is asked before every request, a fallback's included.
4. **The classifier reads every own Bitcoin address of the account**, so a spend's legs sit on its first own input's address and the change to a second own address is no movement. A change card is a legless `unknown` group with `unregistered_output`; a reading with no movement (foreign inputs, nothing moved) is a legless `unknown` group with a plain question, so a person sees it. Another wallet's own address counts as answered: it is never this account's change.
5. **„Nein, Empfänger“ is stored as D185 amended 16 Nov 2026 says**: an event, no migration. The sync reads the account's answers once per run into `ClassifyContext.answered_payee` (a predicate over the HMAC; one field with a default added to `ClassifyContext`, before the EVM wiring's `exchange_hashes`, one argument added to `sync._context` and its call). The answer re-reads the spend from a stored original of the account, checks the output is one the card asks about (`wallet.change_answered` otherwise), records the answer and writes the re-derived spend through the sync's own writer, which supersedes the card: a narrower card while another output is unanswered, else a transfer, routed as the sync routes it. Every other open card of the account whose reading the answer changes is re-derived in the same transaction (the first sync writes one card per past spend, often to the same payee); a card whose spend is in no stored original is left as it is.
6. **D179 and the HMAC.** D179 (2) called `address_masked` „the only form that leaves the table“, while migration 0041's comment on `wallet_address` says what leaves it carries the masked form *or the HMAC*. The answer's event carries a payee's HMAC (no `wallet_address` row is involved). 7. **One host per run, and an empty page after an anchor is confirmed** (review of PR #387, D182 (4) amended). The sync's Esplora client is pinned to the first host that answers, so a run's window and pages come from one index. A host whose final height is below what an earlier run read, or whose newest page fails after its tip, hands the run to the next host with the tip read again there; when no host has reached the stored height the balance is refused (a failed run), never a figure at a lower height. An anchored page that comes back empty ends the walk only when the same host confirms the anchor (`/tx/<txid>/status`); otherwise the walk starts again from that host's newest page down to the cursor's height, and the movements already stored are read again and kept once by their hash. Before, a host that did not know the anchor (one behind, or another than the one that wrote the cursor) moved the cursor past transactions for good.
8. **Legs for a question card supersede it** (review of PR #387). When an output's address is registered and its first sync reads the spend with legs, the card is superseded by the group derived now (a transfer once nothing is unknown); the legs no longer join the legless card, which had kept its question over booked legs. A group that already has legs is joined as before (Solana and EVM rely on it).
9. **Two answers on one account run one after the other** (review of PR #387). `answer_payee` takes the wallet account's row lock (`services/wallets`) before it reads the open cards, so a second answer reads the cards the first wrote; before, it read from a snapshot taken before them and left a narrower card naming an address it had just answered.

**Sync follow-ups (merged and deployed 16 Nov 2026, PR #397, images e73578f4a20e, no migration; this said „not merged“ until then).** Five follow-ups the reviews of the three wirings (PRs #386, #387, #385) left open, each with a test that failed before its fix (item 4 needed no fix), amended by the review of the branch itself (PR #397) in items 1–3, with one open item (6). 1. **A failed balance read keeps the run's pages** (EVM wiring item 4). When the balance read after the history fails (refused, throttled or failed), `services/chain/sync._read` keeps what it read: the groups and movements are written, the original is stored, the cursor moves, and the row is `succeeded` with `balances`, `reconciled` and `difference` null, so a balance never read never reads as reconciled. The code is on `SyncOutcome.error_code`, the log line (`wallet_sync_balance_unread`) and the `wallet.sync_completed` event's `error_code` (an existing field; `deferred` false); the row cannot carry it, since 0041 ties `error_code` to the failed statuses, and a failed status would leave the cursor where it was (`_last_cursor` reads succeeded rows only). The next run starts at the tip and reads the balance again. Two consequences: `GET /wallets/{id}/syncs` labels such a row `deferred` (it reads every succeeded row without balances so; the route is the wallet screens' to change), and the address's first sync (D186 (1)) is still ahead until a run reads its balance, so what the next run reads waits for a person too. **Only when the run stored something new** (review of PR #397): a movement, a question card or an original. The sweep lists one, serves its transactions (the address's own listing waits until every one is swept), then the next. The cursor gains a five-part form `<slot>:<signature>;<closed>;<index>;<at>;<prefix>`: the accounts before `<index>` are complete below `<prefix>` (never below `<slot>`), the one at `<index>` below `<at>`, the later ones and any found in the run below `<closed>`; the three-part form reads as index 0 with `<at>` equal to `<closed>`, and a complete run that swept them all returns to the plain form. It never names a token account, only a number (at most 140 characters with 10-digit slots and an 88-character signature, under the core's 200, beyond which the core fails the run). When the address has been served past `<prefix>` the prefix joins the accounts swept again from there, so no stored account is ever taken to be complete where it is not. **Limits, as built:** the floor is the start (with one listing per open token account), one listing and the transactions of one slot of one account (`<at>` has no signature, so a slot is served whole); pinned at 9 requests for 30 closed accounts, for one account with 60 transactions and for the 306-transaction history, 10 for the mixed one. The index relies on the stored originals never losing a record before a newer one; a deleted original would shift it and a stored account could be taken as swept. **Wallet screens and cards (merged and deployed 16 Nov 2026, PR #398, images 0afac0d2659e, no migration; this said „not merged“ until then).** What makes the deployed sync usable from the app. No migration.

- **The wallet page** (Konten › Wallet). Each address's „Stand“ is its last read in plain German with „Was tun“ (`GET /wallets/{id}/state`, `services/wallet_cards.wallet_state`): not yet read, being read (deferred), reconciled, waiting for an answer, read with another address (Bitcoin's account scope), different, coins left, failed, throttled, refused, key missing, no source yet, retired. A missing key or RPC URL names its Scaleway container variable (`HAUSBUCH_ETHERSCAN_KEY`, `HAUSBUCH_BSC_RPC_URL`), never a value or URL. „Bestand laut Blockchain“ lists per asset the chain's balance at the last read beside what Hausbuch books (the balance less the stored difference; „–“ while the run waits for an answer), with „Jetzt abrufen“ (the existing `POST /wallets/{id}/sync`).
- **Transactions to confirm or answer** (`GET /wallets/{id}/groups`, `POST /groups/{gid}/confirm`, `POST /groups/{gid}/answer`, `services/wallet_groups.py`). Every live group with a live proposed leg is a card, questions first: a group still proposed, and also a confirmed group to which a later sync added a proposed leg (a Bitcoin output to an address registered after the answer); the listing, the counts on the wallet page and in Zu prüfen, confirm and answer all use that one test. „Bestätigen“ keeps a group whose proposed legs all have a kind and that asks nothing. The answers are offered only where they fit the proposed legs (D183 (5)): all out → „Bezahlung“ (disposal); all in → „Geschenk oder Zahlung erhalten“ (acquisition, with an optional cost in euros for a single moving leg, stored as a negative `eur_amount` like cash; no typed cost stays NULL, which means unknown, so the lots price it by the day or show „Kurs fehlt“ (D188), and a typed 0 is 0), „Belohnung“ (staking reward), and for a token the registry does not list and the household does not hold „Spam“. An answer supersedes the group and its live legs with new `source = user` rows that keep the address and index; the old chain rows keep their hashes, so a re-sync writes nothing twice. The answer changes the proposed legs only: a leg already confirmed, and a fee leg, keep their kind and status, so a proposed leg is never confirmed without a person.
- **The Bitcoin change card** (D185 (2)). `GET /groups/{gid}/change` re-reads the spend from the stored original and lists the outputs it asks about. „Nein, Empfänger“ (`POST .../change/payee`) is `change_card.answer_payee`. „Ja, eigene Adresse“ (`POST .../change/own`, `change_card.answer_own_change`) takes the wallet lock, registers the output's address as a `change` address of the same wallet through `services/wallets.register_change_address` (the add path's validation, taken checks, insert and `wallet.address_added` event, factored into `_insert_address`), queues its first sync, re-classifies the spend (a transfer once nothing is unknown), supersedes the card and re-derives the account's other open cards. A missing original is `wallet.original_missing`.
- **Spam** (D187 (3)). The verdict is `asset.spam`, flipped with an `asset.updated` event. `GET /wallets/{id}/hidden-tokens` lists the hidden tokens; „Kein Spam“ (`POST /assets/{id}/promote`) re-proposes the token's spam receipts as proposed airdrops and nothing else. The „Spam“ answer is neither offered nor accepted for a native coin, a token the registry lists (`rules/chain/spam.listed`) or one with any live confirmed movement that is not spam (`wallet.answer_unfit`): a dust receipt of a held token would otherwise hide the whole position; such a receipt is answered like any receipt. Where „Spam“ fits, it marks every live receipt group of the token, as demote does, so no card stays open for a hidden token. Demote and promote touch only the token's incoming airdrop, uncategorised or spam legs (D187 (3)): never a swap's, an acquisition's or a disposal's leg and never another asset's leg; a group with no such leg keeps its rows. **Demote is API-only** (`POST /assets/{id}/demote`): no button calls it, and unlike the answer it is not refused on a listed or held token, so it hides such a position until „Kein Spam“.
- **Zu prüfen.** A new section „Wallets“ with six kinds: `wallet_groups` (one card per wallet counting its proposed transactions, linking to the wallet page), `wallet_change` (the change card's buttons in place), `wallet_question` (another question), `wallet_sync` (a failed run whose source failed, a refused run or a missing key; a throttled run, which resumes by itself the next day, and a run that failed inside Hausbuch, `wallet.sync_error`, which no click fixes, show on the wallet page only), `wallet_unreconciled`, `wallet_coins_left`, each with what to do. `GET /inbox` now takes the settings, to see a missing key.
- **„Antwort zurücknehmen“** (`GET /wallets/{id}/answered`, `POST /groups/{gid}/undo`). The wallet page lists, folded, the last 50 groups a person answered or confirmed (a `movement_group.confirmed` event with reason `routing.person`). Taking an answer back is append-only: a new group supersedes the answered one with the kind the sync first gave it and each leg's first kind and euro amount, every leg proposed, and a `movement_group.proposed` event with reason `routing.person_undone`; the card shows again and the answer stays in the history. A leg the sync first read as spam, shown again with „Kein Spam“, goes back to the airdrop promote made of it, never to a proposed spam leg. Refused for a group holding a spam leg (use „Kein Spam“) and while a current transfer link touches its legs (`wallet.answer_unfit`), and for a group nobody answered. The groups a spam verdict marks (demote, promote, the „Spam“ answer's other receipts) are recorded under `routing.person_spam`, so they are never listed as answered.
- The payment and receipt cards say that a transfer to or from an own exchange or wallet is best left open for now, since linking it comes with step 5. After „Jetzt abrufen“ the page re-reads the state every 4 seconds until each queued address has a newer run (about 3 minutes at most), then reloads the cards.

No full address, transaction hash, URL or key in any answer; every query runs in the workspace's session. The sync's write takes the wallet's account row lock before it reads the live group, the lock an answer takes first, so a sync never adds a leg under a group an answer has just superseded (review finding 3). **Step 5, chain transfers (merged and deployed 16 Nov 2026, PR #396, images 1ce90108542c, no migration; this said „not merged“ until then).** `services/chain/transfers.py` runs the pure matcher (`rules/chain/match_transfer.match`, PR #367) over every open coin leg of a workspace and writes `transfer_link` rows of kind `chain`, `bridge` and `wrap` (D184). No migration. Built in six commits on the branch (10126d74 the service with its cards and the link by hand, 4ecf8c8e the hooks and the command, 5eb29c10 the transfer card on Zu prüfen, 2fbb4000 no dead-end card on a balance-only chain, ad1e854f the actions on a card without a candidate, 78440b18 `types.ts` regenerated), then the fixes from the review of PR #396 (7a938967: a pair links only when its units fit, bridges and wraps wait for `chain_pair`, `unlink` for coin legs; 69e8cd29: a dead matcher link returns what it confirmed to proposed; and the commits after them: a person's pick never reopened, these notes); this entry said „three commits“ until then, when the branch held six.

1. **What a coin leg is.** A live, non-zero movement of a crypto asset that is not spam, on an `exchange` or `wallet` account, of a transfer kind (`internal_transfer_out`/`in`, `exchange_transfer_out`/`in`, or `uncategorized` on a wallet). An exchange row needs a transfer kind. A wallet row needs a live movement group of kind `transfer`, `exchange_transfer`, `wrap` or `bridge`. A chain token's quantity is compared under its `priced_as` symbol (a WETH as ETH, an Arbitrum ETH as ETH), from the asset's twin or the registry. A leg with no such symbol is skipped.
2. **When code writes a link (`matched_by = matcher`).** The matcher finds exactly one counterpart, no other open send would also fit that counterpart, and the units fit (D184 (1)(a), from the review of PR #396): for a `chain` pair, the sent units less the exchange's `fee_quantity` equal the received units within the chain's dust (one smallest unit), also under one hash. A hash names the transaction, not what each own address got of it: 1.0 sent and 0.4 received under one hash (0.6 to a third party in the same transaction, or a second own receipt) is no transfer of 1.0, and until the review code linked and confirmed such a pair. A bridge's units are the matcher's fee-share fit, a wrap's its unit fit. Then one of two things holds:
   - Both legs are already confirmed. For a bridge or a wrap only when the workspace's `chain_pair` holds the pair (D186 (1)); a pair the registry alone knows waits for a person's pick even with both legs confirmed. Until the review a confirmed bridge or wrap was linked by code.
   - The link is of kind `chain`, the two legs share the transaction hash, and code may confirm each proposed leg. Code may confirm a leg when it is an exchange's transfer movement, or when its group is a `transfer` or `exchange_transfer` group that is still proposed, holds no question, was routed `routing.no_match`, and has no other non-fee leg (D186 (1)) but the SOL rent of a token account the send opened (an `airdrop` leg of index `rent`, `rules/chain/solana`). The legs, the group's proposed fee legs and the group are then confirmed in the same transaction (`movement.confirmed`, `movement_group.confirmed` with reason `deterministic`); a group holding a rent leg stays proposed with it, for a person on the wallet's page, while its token leg is confirmed and linked. Tests built from the classifiers' own output: a SOL send between own wallets, a 25 USDC send that opens the receiver's token account, and a Bitcoin send between own wallets link and confirm; the same SOL shape with 0.6 of it to a third party links nothing.

   Everything else waits for a person:
   - a first sync (`routing.chain_first_sync`);
   - any bridge or wrap whose pair `chain_pair` does not hold (since the review of PR #396; „a bridge or a wrap with a proposed leg“ until then), which today is every one;
   - a pair under one hash whose units do not fit (the `partial` card below);
   - a fit by quantity alone with a proposed leg;
   - two fits.
3. **Cards (`GET /transfers/open`, after phase 6's EUR cards).** A fit that code may not link becomes a card:
   - `proposed`, with its one candidate;
   - `ambiguous`, with every candidate. Each candidate carries the `link_kind` its link takes.
   - `partial` (`transfer.partial`, added by the review of PR #396), when the transaction's hash names own legs of other units: part of the coins went to another address, or one send paid several own receipts (one net out leg of 1.0 against receipts of 0.3 and 0.7). The candidates are shown with their quantities beside the leg's, with a plain reason, and no pick is offered, since no link of them is right; „Konto öffnen“ leads to the wallet. **The answer for it is not built:** the wallet card that would split such a send into its transfer part and its payment (or record one send paying several own receipts) is a known gap, so such a send waits on this card.
   - `unmatched`, only for an outgoing leg (`internal_transfer_out`) whose 48-hour window has passed and whose counterpart could be in the app. That is a wallet leg, or an exchange withdrawal whose network names a chain on which the workspace holds an active address (or any address when it names no network). A balance-only chain is excluded (BNB Smart Chain, D181 (c)): no movement could ever pair with the withdrawal, so the card would be a dead end, and the sync's balance check shows the unexplained deposit instead.

   The card shows the account, the date and the signed quantity with its symbol, never an address or a hash. A coin card without a candidate offers „Wallet anlegen oder abrufen“ (Konten) and „Konto öffnen“ (the leg's account, where an exchange's movement is answered). `POST /transfers` with two movement ids of which one is a crypto asset goes to `chain_transfers.link_by_hand`. That function:
   - accepts only a pair the matcher fits with that kind, and refuses any other pair (`transfer.legs_invalid`): another hash, the wrong kind, a quantity that does not fit without a hash, or the wrong window;
   - refuses a pair under one hash whose units do not fit (`transfer.units_differ`, 422, from the review of PR #396: until then a person's pick could link it), with the German text that part of the coins went to another address in the same transaction and that it is answered on the wallet's page;
   - confirms both legs and their group;
   - writes the link with `matched_by = user`.
4. **Currentness on read, never `superseded_by`.** A coin link counts only while both its movements are live (`superseded_by IS NULL`), whoever wrote it. Phase 6's `_current_links` now treats the kinds `chain`, `bridge` and `wrap` as it treats a returned payment's link. So re-booking a leg ends the link without a write, and the leg is open again.

   **What a dead link confirmed goes back to proposed (review of PR #396).** A matcher's link records on its `transfer.linked` event the movements and chain groups it confirmed (`confirmed_movements`, `confirmed_groups`, ids only; empty on a person's link and on a link whose legs were confirmed already). At the start of every `relink`, for each matcher coin link that is no longer current (a leg re-booked, or the link replaced by `POST /transfers/{id}/unlink`), each side whose leg is still live and in no current link has its recorded rows superseded by proposed copies (`movement.superseded`, then `movement.proposed`), and its group, when the link confirmed it and no leg of it is in another current link, by a proposed copy (`movement_group.proposed`, reason `routing.no_match`, as a sync writes it) holding copies of all its live legs. A side a person answered (its leg superseded), a person's own link, and a leg that was ever in a person's link (current or not: their pick confirmed it too) reopen nothing. A reopened send shows again as a card; a reopened receipt among the wallet's open groups (a receipt gets no card). So the reopening happens at the next relink (the next sync, import or answer), not at the moment the link ends.

   For the lots agent: a reopened chain row is a new `asset_movement` row with the same quantity, asset, address, index, tx_hash and occurred_at, status `proposed`, a new `group_id` when its group was copied, and a `movement_hash` of its own (`sha256("<old hash>:reopened:<new id>")`, since 0041's unique index spans superseded rows). The superseded original keeps the sync's hash, and `sync._stored_hashes` reads every row of the account, superseded ones too, so a re-sync finds it and writes nothing new. An exchange row is copied without a `movement_hash`, as `services/exchange/imports._supersede` writes its corrections.

6. **The `transfer_link` contract the lots read.** Column by column:
   - `kind` is `chain`, `bridge` or `wrap`;
   - `out_movement_id` is the sending movement and `in_movement_id` the receiving one, both `asset_movement` ids;
   - `out_transaction_id` and `in_transaction_id` are NULL;
   - `matched_by` is `matcher` or `user`;
   - `created_at` is the command's time;
   - `superseded_by` is set only by `POST /transfers/{id}/unlink`, a person's replacement, which takes coin legs since the review of PR #396 (this said „never set by this step“ until then); the matcher never sets it.

   Each write appends `transfer.linked` (kind, matched_by, both movement ids, no address or hash; since the review also `confirmed_movements` and `confirmed_groups`, the ids a matcher's link confirmed, item 4). The fee stays on the sender: an exchange row's `fee_quantity`, or a wallet group's `fee` movement.

   **Which rows are current.** A link is current while both of its movements are live. A plain `WHERE superseded_by IS NULL` on `transfer_link` also returns dead coin links whose leg was re-booked. A leg that is re-booked and then linked again leaves its old row in the table beside the new one. So a reader must use phase 6's `services.transfers._current_links` / `_linked_ids`, or join both movement ids to `asset_movement` with `superseded_by IS NULL`.
